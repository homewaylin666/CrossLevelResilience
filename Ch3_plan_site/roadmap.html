Roadmap

The concrete scientific question

Do lower-level resilience mechanisms lead, filter, and explain community resilience during a shared ENSO press?

The three verbs are three separate, testable claims — and they make different demands on the data:

VerbClaimReplication it needsFeasibility here
Filterdensity dependence & interactions determine which lower-level responses pass upwardacross species & space (cross-sectional)Strong — hundreds of species × thousands of spatial units
Explainlower-level resilience statistically accounts for community resilienceacross species & spaceStrong
Leadlower levels respond before higher levels in timemultiple time points / eventsWeaker — see the pivot below
★ The key design pivot: replicate across space & species, not (only) time

You correctly noted there is essentially one fully cross-scale-resolved event (2015–16). The way out is to stop treating this as a short time series and start treating it as a massively replicated dose–response within one event. BCI's power is N, not T:

  • Spatial gradient of ΔK. The SWP map gives ~200 sites (and an interpolatable surface) of local drought intensity across the 50 ha. One event becomes thousands of spatially-replicated "mini-experiments": local drought → local individual response → local population response → local community change.
  • Species as replicates. 62–400 species, each with its own sensitivity, let "does population resilience predict community contribution?" be tested across species, not across time.
  • Earlier droughts for the slow levels. 1982–83 and 1997–98 give the community/population levels multiple events — use them to corroborate the slow-level pattern even though matching individual data start only in 2001.

So filter and explain become the primary, well-powered tests; lead becomes a supporting test, run where resolution allows (seedlings 2014→2016→2018; dendrometer 2015–2020) rather than the load-bearing claim.

Phase 0 — the feasibility / coverage audit (do this first)

Why this is its own phase, not a footnote

Before any modelling, we must establish which species, which intervals, and which spatial units actually carry enough signal at all three levels to support a cross-scale claim. This single audit defines the entire analyzable design and de-risks everything after it. Concretely, build a coverage matrix: for each candidate species, how many individuals across how many censuses and how many quadrats; which 20×20 m units have SWP + seedlings + recruitment + adult stems together; which drought × level × resilience-component cells are populated. Output = the shortlist of species, units and intervals the chapter can honestly use. Confidence this is essential: very high.

Step sequence

  1. Phase 0 — coverage audit. Build the species × interval × spatial-unit feasibility matrix; produce the analyzable shortlist.
  2. Confirm spatial nesting in code — seedling plots, the 200 SWP sites, and tree stems on the shared 50-ha grid — so the joins are exact.
  3. Define disturbance & moving baseline — formalise the 2015–16 (and earlier) windows and an AEDT-style expected trajectory; assign each level the resilience component it can support (community 2015–16 = resistance; recovery from dendrometer 2015–2020 or earlier droughts).
  4. (Exploratory) probe species-level leverage — whether any species disproportionately affect community resilience (see note below); secondary to steps 3 & 5, not a gate on them.
  5. Measure resilience at each level with estar; quantify conspecific NDD (j1) and the interspecific interaction matrix (j2).
  6. Test filter → explain → lead across the spatial & species replication, with the temporal lead test where resolution allows.
On the species-leverage branch — demoted, and to be handled carefully

This is no longer a primary aim. Three reasons for caution: (1) conceptual fit — keystone logic comes from low-diversity systems; in a ~300-species, high-evenness forest, community resilience may be distributed rather than species-driven, and finding that would itself be informative. (2) label — abundance/basal area identifies dominant / foundation species, not keystones; only effect on community resilience (matrix leverage / per-capita interaction centrality) approximates keystone-ness. (3) circularity — if species are selected by their effect on community resilience and then tested for that effect, the test is circular; selection must use independent, pre-disturbance criteria. If pursued, treat it as a bounded, clearly-caveated extension.
