Three guiding principles

Your conceptual additions — the assumptions that shape how the disturbance and the cross-scale couplings are modelled.

1Disturbance does not hit every level at once — but it always pushes from the bottom up.

A community-level effect implies the disturbance already acted (directly or first) on individuals and populations, then propagated. Different stressors have different entry levels: a single hunting event enters at the individual level only; a species-specific parasite at individual + population; an invader at individual + population + community. Many environmental presses (incl. ENSO drought) enter mainly through individuals and propagate upward — the ecosystem-level signal is, in effect, the community response built on the individual response.

↳ classify stressors by target × ecological scale rather than source — Simmons et al. 2021, Nat. Ecol. Evol.

2Cross-scale resilience is bidirectional — upper levels feed back down.

The canonical case is density dependence: once population density changes, individuals change because of that density. Crucially, density is an emergent, higher-level variable — an individual dying does not by itself signal changed conditions to its neighbours; the effect exists only once we rise to the population level ("density"), and only then descends back to the individual. So the individual→population link is a two-way coupling, not a one-way sum.

↳ population structure & stage-specific interactions are decisive for community stability — Giménez-Romero et al. 2025, Ecol. Lett.

3All measured change is conditioned on an external disturbance — but some vital-rate change is autonomous.

Our resilience metrics assume an external press. Yet some organisms change vital rates with no external trigger (ants re-engineering their nest; expanding or invasive populations whose rates drift as they spread). That is better seen as the life-stage schedule of the whole unit — vital rates changing intrinsically through time, structurally like a matrix population model. Practically a boundary condition: separate disturbance-driven change from this autonomous / successional drift, i.e. measure resilience as deviation from an expected (moving) baseline, not a static one.

↳ baseline = a moving, environmentally-determined trajectory (AEDT) — Chesson 2017, PLoS Biol.; life-history-driven intrinsic dynamics — Jops et al. 2025, PNAS.
