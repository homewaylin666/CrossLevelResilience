Chapter 3 · Research plan

Cross-scale resilience at Barro Colorado Island

How does community-level resilience arise from the levels below it? This plan tests that question directly with long-term, individual-resolution data from the BCI 50-ha plot, using repeated ENSO droughts as a shared natural disturbance.

Hypothesis-led & data-first · target journal Nature Ecology & Evolution · updated 19 June 2026
Community-level resilience is not a unitary property of the assemblage, but a structured outcome of resilience mechanisms operating at lower organisational levels — where faster, lower-level responses constitute the buffering source for slower, higher-level state variables.

★ The question, made concrete

Do lower-level resilience mechanisms lead, filter, and explain community resilience during a shared ENSO press? With one fully cross-scale-resolved event (2015–16), the design's power comes from space & species, not time: the soil-water-potential map turns the single drought into thousands of spatially-replicated dose–responses, and hundreds of species act as replicates. Filter and explain become the well-powered primary tests; lead is a supporting test where resolution allows. This also occupies a clean niche — Jops et al. 2025 modelled this same BCI data without disturbance. See the roadmap →
